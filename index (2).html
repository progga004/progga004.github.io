About

Teaching vision models to find where something is wrong when the label only says that it is.

Dense, pixel-level annotation is expensive in the domains I care about most. A retina specialist cannot outline every capillary, and a produce line cannot hand-mask every bruise. My research asks how far we can get with weak supervision, adapted foundation models such as DINOv2 and SAM, and photorealistic synthetic data.

I am an M.S. student in Computer Science at Columbia, working in Prof. Shree Nayar's CAVE Lab, Prof. Kaveri Thakoor's AI4VS Lab at Columbia Irving Medical Center, and Prof. Corey Toler-Franklin's GLIM Lab. Before Columbia I studied Computer Science and Applied Mathematics at Stony Brook, where I worked on multi-view gaze estimation with Prof. Dimitris Samaras and Prof. Minh Hoai Nguyen.

This fall I am also a Computer Vision & Garment Intelligence Research Intern at Silhouet Technologies.

Ongoing

Weakly supervised defect localization for produce inspection

Finding where fruit is defective when the only label is good or defective. My current focus is a classical computer vision approach that localizes defects from the image signal itself, without relying on learned representations.

Earlier in the project I built a self-referential anomaly detector over DINOv2 patch features with SAM-based mask sharpening and benchmarked it against WinCLIP and AnomalyCLIP. To get ground truth that does not exist in the wild, I also built a procedural Blender pipeline that renders photorealistic apples with pixel-accurate defect masks.

  • Classical CV
  • Anomaly localization
  • DINOv2
  • SAM
  • Blender
AI4VS Lab, CUIMC Advisor: Prof. Kaveri Thakoor Jan 2026 – present

Retinal and brain imaging for Alzheimer's disease

The retina is an accessible window onto the brain. I work on Alzheimer's disease detection from retinal OCTA that accounts for co-occurring conditions, and on combining retinal (OCT) and brain (MRI) imaging so that each modality can inform the other.

  • OCTA
  • OCT

  • Multimodal learning
Central Michigan University Collaborator: Dr. Md Rafiul Kabir 2026

Retinal OCTA phenotyping with LLM reporting

An interpretable pipeline for Alzheimer's-related retinal changes. It segments retinal vessels in OCTA scans, extracts vascular biomarkers such as vessel density and fractal dimension, and pairs them with LLM-generated clinical reports. Published as my first-author paper at IEEE AIbThings 2026.

  • OCTA
  • Vessel segmentation
  • Biomarkers
  • LLM reporting
GLIM Lab, Columbia / Barnard Advisor: Prof. Corey Toler-Franklin Jan 2026 – present

Animal behavior recognition from video

Action recognition and pose estimation for behavioral phenotyping in animal models of Alzheimer's disease and autism. I am co-authoring a survey on adversarial and unsupervised learning for animal behavior classification, where labeled behavior data is scarce and lab conditions vary.

  • Action recognition
  • Pose estimation
  • Adversarial learning
